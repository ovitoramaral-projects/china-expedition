The supplier describes entering Yading, travelling toward the Pearl Lake route and staying overnight in Yading.

The supplier describes Luorong Pasture as an option for a less demanding visit and the Milk Lake/Five-Color Lake hike as a challenging high-altitude extension. Overnight in Daocheng.

**Field-guide lens:** A deliberate choice between a shorter landscape day and a strenuous hike. Confirm route, altitude, weather, access and the group's condition with the guide; do not treat the longest route as mandatory.

### Day 7 — Friday, November 13
**Daocheng → Shangri-La**

The supplier estimates approximately seven hours of driving. Overnight in Shangri-La.

Supplier-listed visits: Songzanlin Monastery, Dukezong Ancient Town and Napa Lake (seasonal wetland). Overnight in Shangri-La.

### Day 9 — Sunday, November 15
**Shangri-La → Tiger Leaping Gorge → Lijiang**

The supplier schedules viewpoints at Tiger Leaping Gorge before continuing to Lijiang. It notes an optional airport transfer for one traveller.

### Day 10 — Monday, November 16
**Jade Dragon Snow Mountain → Blue Moon Valley → Yuhu Village; Shuhe optional**

The supplier itinerary includes the Glacier Park cable car to approximately 4,506 m and Blue Moon Valley; Yuhu Village is planned, with Shuhe Ancient Town optional if time permits.

**Field-guide lens:** Glacier and snow-peak views, meltwater colour, village architecture and the effects of tourism. The newer plan prefers a viewpoint-led day and does not automatically assume the gondola; confirm which version is intended.

### Day 11 — Tuesday, November 17
**Lijiang → Shibaoshan → Shaxi**

The supplier describes sandstone peaks, forested hills and Buddhist grottoes at Shibaoshan, followed by Shaxi's old market square, courtyards and lanes.

**Field-guide lens:** Stone carving, religious art, historic trade routes and the quieter rhythms of a market town.

### Day 12 — Wednesday, November 18
**Shaxi → Xizhou → Zhoucheng → Dali Old Town**

Supplier-listed stops include Xizhou's Bai architecture and village lanes, and Zhoucheng's tie-dye craft tradition, before Dali Old Town.

**Field-guide lens:** Bai cultural landscapes, architecture, markets, textile craft and the distinction between observing a craft and interrupting the artisan's work.

### Day 13 — Thursday, November 19
**Erhai Lake cycling → high-speed train to Kunming**

The supplier describes a relaxed lakeside route through villages and farmland, followed by the train to Kunming.

**Field-guide lens:** Agricultural life, lakeshore ecology, village-to-city transitions and small details noticed at cycling pace. Confirm cycling distance, weather, train time and luggage arrangements.

### Day 14 — Friday, November 20

The supplier schedules the Stone Forest, a karst landscape, followed by Dounan Flower Market. Overnight in Kunming.

**Field-guide lens:** Limestone forms, scale and pathways at Shilin; the movement, colour, work and commerce of a major flower market.

## 4. Documentary field-guide approach

The field guide should follow `China Expedition Itinerary.txt` as the current route and use the hotel vouchers to check overnight towns. The original supplier itinerary is a historical alternative. Keep the Nov 12–14 contradiction visible until confirmed. The guide should work as a reusable knowledge base rather than a conventional long essay; describe what can be seen, heard, learned and respectfully photographed, while separating verified facts from interpretation.

### Narrative arc

1. **Arrival and orientation:** Chengdu, the city as a threshold into the expedition.
2. **Entering the highlands:** Siguniangshan, Danba, Bamei and Xinduqiao; mountain environments and settlement patterns.
3. **Kham and high-altitude landscapes:** Tagong, Litang, Daocheng and Yading; pastoral life, sacred landscapes and practical altitude decisions.
4. **Tibetan cultural life in Yunnan:** Shangri-La, Songzanlin, Dukezong and Napa Lake.
5. **Gorges and mountain-town transitions:** Tiger Leaping Gorge, Lijiang and Jade Dragon Snow Mountain.
6. **Tea Horse Road and Bai/Naxi cultural landscapes:** Shibaoshan, Shaxi, Xizhou, Zhoucheng, Dali and Erhai Lake.
7. **Kunming and the journey's close:** Shilin, Dounan Flower Market and departure.

This arc is a legacy supplier-route narrative and should not be used as the current route order. Rebuild the active documentary arc from the current itinerary: Chengdu; Siguniang/Danba; Tagong–Yajiang–Litang; Yading and the unresolved Daocheng/Shangri-La sequence; Deqin/Meili; Shangri-La/Songzanlin; Lijiang; Shaxi; Erhai/Dali; Kunming transfer.

### Each destination entry should contain

- **Orientation:** what the place is and why it matters to this route.
- **What to observe:** landscape, architecture, material culture, work, movement, sound and everyday life.
- **Context:** sourced history, ecology, cultural context and terminology.
- **Photographic approach:** wide / medium / detail opportunities, light, weather, visual relationships and respectful portrait practice.
- **Field prompts:** questions that invite observation rather than predetermined conclusions.
- **Responsible travel:** consent, sacred spaces, environmental care and avoiding disruption.
- **Food and language:** only useful, locally relevant material; distinguish Standard Mandarin from local languages or varieties.
- **Practical notes:** access, altitude, walking demand, seasonality and what must be rechecked.
- **Further exploration:** carefully selected books, films, museum or institutional sources, with bibliographic details.
- **Source notes:** links or citations and confidence labels.

### Sample destination module: Siguniangshan / Shuangqiao Valley

**Role in the journey:** The first mountain chapter in the supplier itinerary. The newer plan places arrival in Chengdu on Nov 7 and the mountain transfer on Nov 8, so the exact day remains subject to route confirmation.

**Visual themes:** mountain scale; layers of ridges; forest against rock; road and valley as lines through the frame; changing cloud and weather; small human figures used to communicate scale without turning local residents into props.

**Observation prompts**
- How does the landscape change between road, forest, open valley and distant peak?
- Which details show weather or altitude more clearly than a dramatic panorama?
- Where does infrastructure guide movement through the landscape?
- What changes when you wait for the light rather than move rapidly between viewpoints?
- If people are present, are they engaged in ordinary life, work or worship? Is a photograph appropriate, and have you asked where needed?

**Responsible practice**
- Follow local instructions and marked access routes.
- Do not leave paths or disturb vegetation for a composition.
- Keep people and religious activity in context; ask permission for identifiable portraits.
- Avoid presenting a local community as a visual “discovery” or treating sacred places as props.
- Confirm November access, shuttle arrangements, opening times, road conditions and weather close to travel.

**Research note:** This is a structured starter module, not the complete researched Siguniangshan chapter. Specific geology, ecology, local history, admission details and seasonal claims need individually sourced verification before publication.

### Required storyboard standards

## 10. Source and confidence rules

1. **Itinerary claims:** use `China Expedition Itinerary.txt` as the current working route; use the hotel vouchers to check overnights; treat the supplier PDF as a historical alternative. Neither itinerary proves current opening hours or access.
2. **Research claims:** prefer official park, museum, government, UNESCO, academic or other primary sources when suitable; use reputable secondary sources for context.
3. **Current conditions:** mark opening hours, transport, permits, weather, road access and prices with a check date and recheck close to departure.
4. **Cultural description:** avoid sweeping claims about an ethnic or religious group; distinguish regional context from what can actually be observed at a particular site.
5. **Interpretation:** label a photographic or documentary interpretation as a suggestion, not a factual claim.
6. **Gaps:** write `needs research` when evidence is absent. Do not fill gaps with plausible-sounding detail.
7. **Citations:** place source references directly beside the claims they support and retain full source metadata in the knowledge base.
