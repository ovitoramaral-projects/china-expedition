Day-by-day itinerary

Day 1 — Saturday, November 7

Chengdu city day

You are already accommodated in Chengdu from the previous night. Keep the day relaxed, explore the immediate neighbourhood and enjoy your first Sichuan meal. The hostel booking continues through the night of November 7, with check-out on November 8.

Focus: Arrival, street life, food and preparation.

Day 2 — Sunday, November 8

Chengdu → Dujiangyan → Wolong → Siguniang

Travel into the mountains via Dujiangyan and Wolong, crossing the Balang Mountain area. Arrive in the Siguniangshan region.

Focus: The transition from the Sichuan basin to the high mountains.

Day 3 — Monday, November 9

Siguniang → Danba

Enjoy the Siguniang mountain scenery before continuing towards Danba. The Shuangqiao Valley scenic area is the principal sightseeing option in this region, with shuttle buses and walking paths.

Focus: Alpine landscapes, mountain villages and the journey into Danba.

Day 4 — Tuesday, November 10

Danba → Jiaju Tibetan Village → Tagong

Visit Jiaju, known for its traditional hillside Tibetan houses, then continue towards Tagong on the Tibetan plateau.

Focus: Jiarong Tibetan architecture, village life and the cultural transition into Kham.

Day 5 — Wednesday, November 11

Tagong → Gunong → Muya Great Temple → Heishicheng → Yajiang

Travel through plateau landscapes and pastoral areas, stopping around Gunong and the Muya Great Temple before continuing through the Heishicheng area to Yajiang.

Tagong Monastery is an important cultural stop to include during your Tagong visit if the schedule permits.

Focus: Tibetan Buddhist life, monasteries, prayer flags, herding culture and highland scenery.

Day 6 — Thursday, November 12

Yajiang → Litang → Changqingchunke’er Temple → Shangri-La

Cross the high plateau via Litang, around 3,950 metres above sea level. Visit Changqingchunke’er Temple before continuing towards Shangri-La.

Focus: High-altitude Tibetan town life, religious practice and the long transition south into Yunnan.

Day 7 — Friday, November 13

Full day at Yading Nature Reserve

Spend the day exploring Yading’s dramatic high-altitude landscape. The exact walking route should be confirmed with your guide.

The shorter Pearl Lake route is the less demanding option. The longer Milk Lake and Five-Color Lake hike is a serious high-altitude outing, typically around 10–11 km round trip and reaching approximately 4,600–4,700 metres.

Focus: Alpine lakes, snow peaks, hiking and the expedition’s major mountain experience.

Day 8 — Saturday, November 14

Shangri-La → Deqin

Travel towards Deqin and the Meili Snow Mountain area. The route offers highland scenery and opportunities to observe the changing landscape.

Focus: Mountain travel and the approach to the eastern Himalaya region.

Day 9 — Sunday, November 15

Deqin → Meili Snow Mountain → Shangri-La

Spend time around the Meili Snow Mountain viewpoints, particularly the Feilai Temple area, before returning to Shangri-La.

Focus: Mountain panoramas, changing light and the relationship between local communities and sacred peaks.

Day 10 — Monday, November 16

Shangri-La → Songzanlin Monastery → Lijiang

Visit Songzanlin Monastery, one of the most important Tibetan Buddhist monasteries in Yunnan, then travel to Lijiang. One traveller is scheduled to be dropped at the airport.

Focus: Monastery architecture, religious life and the transition from Tibetan highlands to Naxi cultural territory.

Day 11 — Tuesday, November 17

Jade Dragon Snow Mountain → Lijiang Ancient Town

Spend the day around Jade Dragon Snow Mountain and Lijiang. The preferred approach is to seek a viewpoint with the mountain behind the town, rather than automatically taking the glacier gondola.

Black Dragon Pool, Wangu Pavilion and the Baisha area are possible viewpoints, depending on weather and the day’s logistics.

Focus: Mountain-and-town compositions, Naxi cultural landscapes and tourism’s influence on the old town.

Day 12 — Wednesday, November 18

Lijiang → Shaxi Ancient Town

Travel to Shaxi, a historic settlement on the Tea Horse Road. Explore its old streets, market square and surrounding village landscape at a slower pace.

Focus: Everyday local life, historic trade routes, traditional architecture and a quieter documentary rhythm.

Day 13 — Thursday, November 19

Shaxi → Erhai Lake → Dali

Continue to the Erhai Lake area and Dali. Depending on timing, explore lakeside villages and the agricultural landscape before settling in Dali.

Focus: Bai cultural landscapes, village life, farming, the lake and the contrast with the Tibetan plateau.

Day 14 — Friday, November 20

Dali → Kunming — expedition ends

Transfer to Kunming in the morning, with arrival expected in the afternoon. This is the final day of the expedition.

Focus: Concluding the journey and transitioning from the highlands and historic towns to a major Yunnan city.

The documentary arc

1. Tibetan highland communities

Danba, Jiaju, Tagong, Muya and Litang — village life, pastoral culture, Buddhist practice and daily routines.

2. Mountains and sacred landscapes

Siguniang, Yading and Meili — the physical scale of the plateau, changing weather and the cultural meaning of mountains.

3. Living Buddhist traditions

Tagong Monastery, Muya Great Temple, Changqingchunke’er Temple and Songzanlin — architecture alongside religious activity and people.

4. Tea Horse Road and Yunnan

Lijiang, Shaxi and Dali — Naxi and Bai cultural landscapes, trade history, village life and contemporary tourism.

Important practical notes

• Weather and roads: November can bring snow, ice and sudden temperature drops on high mountain roads. Keep some flexibility for the plateau driving days.
• Altitude: Yading is the most demanding planned hiking day. Confirm the route with your guide and do not force the long hike if you feel unwell.
• Photography: Prioritise time with people and everyday activities instead of trying to photograph every landmark. Tagong, Litang, Songzanlin and Shaxi are especially useful cultural anchors.
• Jade Dragon Snow Mountain: The gondola remains optional; a mountain-viewpoint day is consistent with the preference to avoid repeating high-altitude tourist experiences.
