The itinerary places “Shangri-La” on Nov 12 and Yading on Nov 13, but the vouchers show Daocheng/Yading-area lodging Nov 12–14 and Deqin on Nov 14. Tiger Leaping Gorge, Shibaoshan, Xizhou, Zhoucheng, Stone Forest, Dounan Flower Market and the Nov 21 departure are from the supplier itinerary and are not confirmed current stops.

# 1. Historical supplier-route reference — Siguniangshan / Shuangqiao Valley
**Original PDF date:** Saturday, 7 November (not the current plan date)

## Location: Mount Siguniang / Siguniangshan
**Chinese:** 四姑娘山  
**Administrative location:** Xiaojin County, Aba Tibetan and Qiang Autonomous Prefecture, Sichuan  
**Role in route:** First major mountain landscape in the PDF itinerary.

### What the place is
Mount Siguniang is a mountain group in western Sichuan. The trip's scenic-area experience is less about reaching a summit and more about seeing the scale of the mountain terrain through valleys, forest, rock, streams and changing weather. The official scenic-area material describes Shuangqiao Valley as a long valley with many named scenic points and shuttle access.

### Named location: Shuangqiao Valley
**Chinese:** 双桥沟  
**Best use of the stop:** A scenic valley visit using the official visitor transport and permitted paths; it is not the same experience as a long independent mountain trek.

**What to look for**
- Layered ridgelines and the way cloud obscures and reveals the peaks.
- Transitions between forest, open slopes, water and exposed rock.
- Bridges, roads and shuttle stops as evidence of how visitors move through the valley.
- Small details—frost, bark, water, wind-shaped vegetation—that communicate conditions better than a postcard panorama.

**Photography plan**
1. **Establishing frame:** a broad valley view with the ridges placed off-centre and enough foreground to show depth.
2. **Layer study:** use the longer end of the zoom range to compress successive ridgelines and passing cloud.
3. **Human scale:** include a person only when naturally present and with appropriate consent; avoid using strangers as anonymous props.
4. **Detail frame:** water, frost, rock texture or a bridge detail to break up the panorama sequence.
5. **Weather frame:** if cloud moves across a peak, hold position and make a short sequence rather than taking only one frame.

**Practical notes**
- Official scenic-area pages list Shuangqiao Valley tickets and sightseeing-bus access; the currently published schedule and prices are not a guarantee for the travel date. Recheck before departure.
- November conditions can change quickly at altitude. Keep gloves and a windproof/waterproof layer accessible, not buried in luggage.
- Do not leave designated paths to improve a composition.
- The 15-day PDF also lists Changping Valley on Day 2; the newer planning draft places Shuangqiao Valley mainly on Nov 9. Confirm which visit actually occurs and when.

**Verified reference**
- Official scenic-area description: https://www.sgns.cn/en/index.php?Itemid=312&option=com_gantry5&view=custom
- Official ticketing information: https://www.sgns.cn/en/index.php?Itemid=379&option=com_gantry5&view=custom
- Geosite description: https://www.sgnsgeopark.cn/en/index.php?Itemid=293&catid=19&id=533%3A2022-01-01-12-00-9&option=com_content&view=article

## Named location: Changping Valley (PDF Day 2)
**Chinese:** 长坪沟  
The 15-day PDF includes Changping Valley before the transfer to Danba. Treat it as a separate visit from Shuangqiao Valley: don't assume one valley's access, walking style or views apply to the other. Confirm the actual route, walking distance, shuttle arrangements and time available with the guide. Official scenic-area ticketing information lists Changping Valley separately from Shuangqiao Valley.

**Research gap:** A detailed trail-by-trail description and November access status should be confirmed against the official park and the guide's current operational information.

# 2. Historical supplier-route reference — Changping Valley → Danba / Zhonglu
**PDF itinerary date:** Sunday, 8 November

## Location: Danba County and town
**Chinese:** 丹巴  
**Administrative location:** Ganzi (Garze) Tibetan Autonomous Prefecture, western Sichuan  
**Role in route:** Base for nearby Jiarong/Rgyalrong Tibetan settlements and historic watchtowers.

Danba is the county and transport hub; Jiaju, Zhonglu and Suopo are separate nearby settlements. They should not be treated as interchangeable names for one attraction. The area's hillside villages are known for stone-and-earth houses, watchtowers and their relationship to valley slopes.

## Named location: Zhonglu Tibetan Village
**Chinese:** 中路藏寨  
**Role in PDF route:** The PDF names Danba/Zhonglu as the Day 2 destination/overnight area.

**What to look for**
- Houses set along the slope rather than a single dense street.
- The relationship between domestic architecture, terraces, orchards and the valley.
- Watchtowers viewed in relation to ordinary houses and the landscape, not only as isolated objects.
- Changes in light on the hillside toward late afternoon, if the schedule allows.

**Photography plan**
- Make one wide frame that describes the village's position in the landscape.
- Photograph architectural details from public paths and with permission where a home or courtyard is involved.
- Avoid entering homes, courtyards or tower structures unless explicitly invited.
- A slower walk and notes about sounds, work and movement will usually be more revealing than a rapid sequence of portraits.

**Access note:** Travel sources place Zhonglu several kilometres northeast of Danba town; actual road time depends on the route and conditions. Check with the driver rather than relying on generic taxi estimates.

## Named location: Suopo Watchtowers
**Chinese:** 梭坡古碉  
Suopo is particularly associated with clusters of stone watchtowers. The towers are part of a lived cultural landscape and may be near private property. Do not climb, enter or touch a tower without explicit permission.

**Look for**
- The tower's vertical silhouette against the hillside.
- Differences in form and position between towers and domestic buildings.
- How the river valley and settlement geography explain why towers occupy particular slopes.

## Named location: Jiaju Tibetan Village
**Chinese:** 甲居藏寨  
The newer itinerary explicitly lists Jiaju on Nov 10, while the 15-day PDF foregrounds Danba/Zhonglu and does not clearly list Jiaju in the same day sequence. It is a strong candidate for a Danba-area stop, but its place in the final route must be confirmed.

**What to observe**
- Whitewashed walls and coloured details near rooflines.
- The stepped distribution of houses along the slope.
- Terraced fields, orchards and the mountain/valley backdrop.
- The difference between a designated viewpoint and the quieter lived spaces within the village.

**Responsible travel:** This is a home landscape, not a film set. Ask before photographing residents, dress-up activities, interiors or family courtyards. Avoid photographing children close-up without guardian consent.

**Verified references**
- Danba village overview, distinguishing Jiaju, Zhonglu and Suopo: https://www.travelchinaguide.com/cityguides/sichuan/garze/danba-tibetan-villages.htm
- Recent location/context guide with coordinates and village distinctions: https://www.fieldguidetochina.com/places/danba/
- Danba map and relative location information: https://www.sichuantravelguide.com/danba/danba-maps.html

# 3. Historical supplier-route reference — Danba → Bamei → Xinduqiao
**PDF itinerary date:** Monday, 9 November

This is a multi-stop road day. Exact order and dwell time should be agreed with the guide; don't assume every stop can be explored at length.

## Named location: Bamei
**Chinese commonly used:** 八美  
**Role:** A settlement/landscape area on the route west from Danba toward the Tagong/Xinduqiao region.

**Field approach**
- Treat Bamei as a geographic transition and a chance to observe changing terrain, villages and roadside land use.
- Ask the guide which exact viewpoint or attraction is intended: the itinerary name alone does not specify a single gate, trail or landmark.
- Avoid assigning a particular geological formation or attraction to this stop until the operator confirms the exact location.

## Named location: Ink Stone Park / Moshi Park
**Chinese:** 墨石公园  
**Role:** PDF-listed scenic stop near the Bamei route.

**What to observe**
- The contrast between dark, sculptural rock forms and the surrounding plateau.
- How the visitor route frames the formations; compare close textures with wider patterns.

# 20. Expanded destination notes: what to do, eat, learn and watch out for

This section adds the missing practical depth: a realistic visit plan, local food to look for, historical context, and destination-specific cautions. Restaurant names and opening hours can change; where a venue is not supported by a reliable current source, the guide recommends a food or market area and a dish rather than inventing a business.

## 20.1 Siguniangshan / Rilong town and Shuangqiao Valley

### What to do
- **Shuangqiao Valley:** use the official sightseeing bus to cover the longer valley, then spend your time at the stops that give different kinds of views—open mountain profiles, forest, water and close rock textures. Ask the guide which stops are open and how long the bus waits.
- **Rilong / Siguniangshan town:** use the town for a short evening walk, a meal and preparation for the next day's outing. This is a mountain gateway, not a place to schedule a late night before a cold outdoor day.
- **If the 15-day PDF's Changping Valley visit is confirmed:** ask whether the plan is a short scenic visit or a longer walk. Do not assume it is interchangeable with Shuangqiao.

### Food to look for
In the mountain towns of western Sichuan, look for hot noodle soups, yak or beef dishes, Tibetan-style momos/dumplings, warming hotpot, and simple stir-fried vegetables with rice. Menus and quality vary; ask the driver or hotel for a currently open family restaurant near the actual accommodation. A hot meal and warm drink are especially practical after a cold day. Do not assume every “yak” menu item is locally sourced.

### Context worth knowing
The Siguniangshan area sits at a meeting point of high mountain ecosystems and Tibetan/Qiang cultural landscapes. Its value is not just the mountain silhouette: valley routes, grazing areas, settlement patterns and seasonal weather shape how people move through the region. The park's own ticketing page treats Shuangqiao, Changping and Haizi valleys as distinct visitor areas, with different ticket and access arrangements.

### Risks and care
- The official park notice lists entry windows of 08:00–15:00 for Shuangqiao Valley and 08:00–14:30 for Changping and Haizi valleys in the notice effective from April 1, 2026; recheck for later changes before travel.
- Cold, wind, snow and ice can arrive quickly. Use shoes with grip and keep gloves, hat and waterproof/windproof layers accessible.
- Do not leave marked paths for a better frame or stand on frozen edges.
- If headache, nausea, unusual fatigue, dizziness or breathlessness develops at altitude, tell the guide rather than pushing on.
- Official reference: https://www.sgnsgeopark.cn/en/index.php?id=13&limitstart=45 and https://www.sgns.cn/en/index.php?Itemid=379&option=com_gantry5&view=custom

## 20.2 Danba, Zhonglu, Suopo and Jiaju

### A practical visit
If the final schedule permits more than one village, prioritize **one village panorama and one closer walk** rather than racing through three identical photo stops.
- **Zhonglu:** hillside stone houses and a broad view of the settlement; some sources describe it as a quieter, dispersed collection of houses rather than a compact “old town.”
- **Suopo:** focus on the watchtower clusters and how they sit among inhabited houses and terraced slopes.
- **Jiaju:** look at the tiered hillside settlement and the relationship between roofs, fields and the valley.
- **Dadu River gorge:** a roadside landscape feature on the wider Danba approach if the driver confirms a safe pull-off.

### History and cultural context
The villages are associated with the **Jiarong/Gyarong Tibetan** cultural region. The stone watchtowers are historical structures within living village environments; their original roles are not fully settled in popular accounts, with defensive use, storage and displays of family status all discussed. Do not present a single explanation as certain. These are not abandoned ruins: they may be on private property and close to family homes.

### Food to look for
Ask for local Sichuan/Tibetan home-style food in Danba town or at the guesthouse: yak or beef stew, noodle soup, dumplings, seasonal vegetables, potatoes and simple rice dishes. If staying in a village guesthouse, ask in advance whether dinner is included and whether they need notice for dietary restrictions. Avoid planning a restaurant detour before confirming the driving time and exact overnight.

### Risks and care
- Do not enter or climb a watchtower unless the owner explicitly invites you and confirms it is safe.
- Ask before photographing people, homes, courtyards or domestic work.
- Roads to hillside villages can be narrow; do not stand in the carriageway to photograph a passing vehicle.
- The valley is lower than Yading, but the trip is still remote enough that delays, limited English and variable mobile coverage can matter.

## 20.3 Bamei, Moshi Park, Huiyuan Monastery, Tagong and Xinduqiao

### What to do
- **Moshi Park / Ink Stone Park (墨石公园):** follow the permitted visitor route through the dark, sculptural formations. Make a wide establishing frame, then study the forms and shadows close-up. Do not climb the formations or leave the boardwalk for a more dramatic angle.
- **Huiyuan Monastery (惠远寺):** treat this as a cultural visit, not merely a building exterior. Observe the courtyard, thresholds and the way people use the space; ask before photographing worshippers or interiors.
- **Tagong grassland (塔公草原):** look for the relationship between grassland, settlement, mountains and prayer flags. Ask the driver which viewpoint or access point is included; the itinerary doesn't specify one.
- **Tagong Monastery (塔公寺):** this appears in the newer planning draft as a possible addition, so confirm that it is included and how much time is available.
- **Xinduqiao (新都桥):** ask the guide to name the exact scenic pull-offs. “Xinduqiao photography paradise” is a broad regional label, not one guaranteed viewpoint.

### Historical context
This stretch crosses the cultural landscape of Kham, with active Tibetan Buddhist institutions and pastoral settlements. A monastery's significance is not limited to its age or visual style: it is part of a living religious network. Moshi Park, by contrast, is primarily a geological landscape stop; avoid inventing a cultural story for a rock formation unless a reliable interpretation panel or guide provides one.

### Food to look for
In Tagong and Xinduqiao, look for Tibetan-style noodle soup, momos, yak or beef dishes, tsampa (roasted barley flour), butter tea or sweet milk tea, and barley-based drinks where offered. Taste butter tea as a local food tradition rather than assuming you must like it. If altitude or a long drive affects appetite, choose a simple hot meal and carry snacks.

### Risks and care
- Wind and exposure can make plateau stops feel much colder than the town temperature suggests.
- Do not approach herds or dogs without asking the herder/owner; give animals space.
- Religious etiquette: walk calmly, follow local direction around prayer objects, and never interrupt prayer for a photograph.
- The itinerary mentions a former residence/summer-palace site associated with the Seventh Dalai Lama but does not identify it precisely. Ask the operator for the Chinese name before planning or interpreting this stop.

## 20.4 Daocheng and Yading: a two-day plan, not a checklist

### Recommended structure if the 15-day PDF's sequence is confirmed
**Day A — shorter route:** reserve shuttle → Zhaguanbeng → Chonggu area/monastery → Pearl Lake → return. Allow time for stops, weather and walking slowly.
**Day B — longer route only if conditions and health permit:** shuttle → Chonggu → electric cart if operating → Luorong Pasture → Milk Lake/Five-Color Lake route → return before the official turn-back time. Confirm current rules at the entrance that morning.

### Why the landscape matters
Yading is centred on three sacred peaks—Xiannairi, Yangmaiyong and Xiaruoduoji—within a Tibetan cultural landscape. The lakes and peaks are not merely scenic targets; they are connected to pilgrimage and religious meaning. Follow local direction around shrines, do not swim in or throw objects into the lakes, and avoid treating prayer flags as disposable decorations.

### Food and supplies
Do **not** rely on a full restaurant meal being available on the trail. Travel guides report limited food options inside the reserve, with snack stalls/simple food rather than a dependable restaurant service. Carry water and a practical packed lunch/snacks from the town or accommodation; confirm what's permitted and available with the guide. Eat a familiar, light breakfast before starting.

### Safety—especially important
- Much of the reserve is above roughly 3,900 m; Milk Lake and Five-Color Lake are around 4,500–4,700 m depending on the exact point and source.
- The longer route is steep and exposed. Report headache, nausea, dizziness, unusual exhaustion or breathlessness immediately. Do not continue upward to “finish the route” if symptoms are worsening.
- Confirm the park's current turn-back time; recent guides report a 14:30 checkpoint/turn-back rule for the high lake route, but the operator must confirm the rule in force for your date.
- Snow or ice can make boardwalks and loose trail surfaces slippery. Use grippy footwear and poles if useful; keep hands free on steep sections.
- Oxygen is not a substitute for descending or obtaining medical help when altitude illness is suspected.
- Keep a warm layer, waterproof shell, gloves, sun protection and spare camera battery accessible. Cold and wind drain batteries.
- The current itinerary and hotel vouchers conflict around Nov 12–14: itinerary text places “Shangri-La” on Nov 12 and Yading on Nov 13, while lodging vouchers show the Daocheng/Yading area through Nov 14. Confirm actual overnight towns and transfers before relying on a day-by-day route.

## 20.5 Shangri-La: monastery, old town, wetland and dinner

### A good use of the 15-day PDF's cultural day
**Morning:** Songzanlin Monastery and, if access and weather allow, the viewpoint around Lamuyangcuo Lake.  
**Midday:** eat in town rather than assuming the monastery grounds provide a full lunch.  
**Afternoon:** Dukezong Ancient Town, including Moonlight Square and the large prayer wheel.  
**Late afternoon:** Napa Lake only if the driver confirms time and a suitable access point; it is a seasonal wetland, not a single guaranteed viewpoint.

### History
Songzanlin Monastery was founded in **1679** under the patronage of the Fifth Dalai Lama and belongs to the Gelug school of Tibetan Buddhism. It is often nicknamed the “Little Potala Palace” because of its hillside architecture. Dukezong is a historic Tibetan settlement; a major fire in 2014 led to substantial rebuilding, so the present town should be understood as a living place with a complicated relationship between heritage, restoration and tourism.

### What to eat
Seek out:
- **Yak hotpot or yak/beef stew** for a warming shared meal.
- **Momos** (dumplings) and noodle soups for a simpler lunch.
- **Tsampa** (roasted barley flour) and **butter tea** or sweet tea if you want to sample Tibetan staples.
- **Highland barley** dishes or drinks where available.
- **Matsutake** dishes when in season and when the restaurant can explain the source; don't assume every menu's “wild mushroom” is matsutake.

A destination guide lists **Potala Wooden Restaurant** near Dukezong's north gate, **Arro Khampa** on Cangfang Road, and **Zhaxi Dele Tibetan Restaurant** near Moon Square. Treat these as leads, not guaranteed current recommendations: verify the business is still operating, check recent reviews and confirm the address in a map app before going. Source: https://www.chinaexpeditiontours.com/china-guide/eating-in-shangri-la.html

### Safety and etiquette
- Shangri-La is around 3,300 m; take the first day gently if you have recently travelled over high passes.
- Monastery stairs can be steep; move slowly and use handrails.
- Follow posted photography rules and ask before taking portraits of monks or worshippers.
- Walk clockwise around prayer wheels and stupas where that is the local convention; follow the flow of worshippers and any posted signs.
- Napa Lake is seasonal. Stay in designated areas and do not approach wildlife or livestock for a photograph.

## 20.7 Lijiang and Jade Dragon Snow Mountain

### Suggested choices—do not try to do everything
**If taking the glacier cable car:** prioritize the mountain area and Blue Moon Valley, then decide whether there is time for Yuhu Village.  
**If skipping the cable car:** choose Black Dragon Pool for a classic mountain reflection when visibility is good, then Baisha or Shuhe for a quieter settlement walk, with Lijiang Old Town in the late afternoon/evening.

### Specific places
- **Black Dragon Pool (黑龙潭):** water, bridges, pavilions and a classic view toward Jade Dragon Snow Mountain. The historic complex dates in part to the Qing period; a source identifies construction/expansion in 1737.
- **Lijiang Old Town / Dayan (丽江古城):** walk Sifang Street and the lanes around it, then leave the busiest commercial streets to see how water channels and residential lanes connect.
- **Mu Mansion (木府):** useful for understanding the Mu hereditary rulers and Lijiang's political history, if time and ticketing allow.
- **Baisha (白沙):** a quieter Naxi settlement area associated with the earlier centre of Mu-family power before the move toward Dayan. Confirm the specific mural/heritage site that the driver intends.
- **Shuhe (束河):** a historic settlement with lanes, springs and courtyard houses; it is still a lived place as well as a visitor destination.
- **Yuhu Village (玉湖村):** the PDF-listed village stop; ask which part of the village is included and whether a local guide is available.

### History to know
Lijiang Old Town became a major centre on the Tea Horse Road, linking Yunnan with Tibetan regions and other trading networks. Its Naxi cultural history includes Dongba traditions. The old town's water system and street layout are part of the heritage, not just decorative scenery. The town is UNESCO-listed, but much of today's visitor experience is also shaped by tourism and rebuilding; observe both.

### What to eat
Look for **Lijiang baba** (a local flatbread/pastry, with sweet and savoury versions), **chicken with matsutake or other mushrooms** where in season and clearly identified, **yak/beef hotpot**, **crossing-the-bridge noodles** if you want a Yunnan classic, and local goat-cheese or dairy products where available. In Xizhou later in the trip, try Xizhou baba again for comparison. Ask for a current recommendation from your hotel and check recent reviews rather than relying on old lists of tourist-town restaurants.

### Safety and care
- Stone lanes can be slippery after rain or frost; shoes with grip are useful.
- Cable-car access is weather-dependent and can involve a rapid move to very high elevation. Confirm the altitude, route and any health advice with the operator.
- Don't assume a mountain view will be visible. Keep a flexible plan with Black Dragon Pool/Baisha as alternatives.
- In old-town lanes, watch for bicycles, scooters and delivery vehicles even where parts are pedestrianized.
- Ask before photographing Dongba rituals, residents or interiors.

## 20.8 Shibaoshan and Shaxi: slow down here

### Shibaoshan
The 15-day PDF lists Shibaoshan before Shaxi. The area is known for temples, forested hills and Buddhist rock carvings. Ask whether the itinerary includes the Shizhongshan Grottoes, a temple, or only a scenic stop; the time and route differ.
- Study carvings from the permitted viewing distance; no touching, rubbing, wetting or flash where prohibited.
- Take time to read official interpretation panels rather than inventing the story of an image.
- Check the exact access route and ticket arrangement with the guide.

### Shaxi: suggested walking sequence
1. **Sideng Market Square (寺登街):** begin with the historic market space and observe how the square is used now.
2. **The old theatre stage:** consider it as part of the square's civic and performance life, not just an architectural photo.
3. **Xingjiao Temple (兴教寺):** a Ming-period Bai Buddhist temple commonly identified as a key historic structure; check current opening and photography rules.
4. **Yujin Bridge and the lanes around the old town:** look at the connection between town, river crossing and travel routes.
5. **A village walk beyond the most commercial lanes:** only with the guide's advice and while respecting private property.

### History
Shaxi was a market settlement on the Tea Horse Road. Its square, temple and theatre make it a particularly legible place to think about the practical infrastructure of trade—where people met, exchanged goods, worshipped and performed. Don't imagine the old route as a single romantic trail; it was a network of regional markets and paths.

### What to eat
Ask for **Shaxi local noodles**, simple rice dishes, seasonal vegetables, and Bai/Yunnan home-style food. A current travel listing describes a Monday market in Shaxi; the PDF itinerary places your overnight visit on Tuesday, 17 November, so don't plan around the Monday market unless the route changes. Confirm market day locally before relying on it. Try a local snack where you can see how it is prepared and ask what it is called.

### Safety and care
- Old paving can be uneven and slippery.
- Keep an eye on motorbikes and local vehicles in lanes.
- Ask before photographing homes, shopkeepers or craft work.
- Check the temple opening and whether a fee is charged; published travel guides differ and may be out of date.

## 20.9 Xizhou, Zhoucheng, Erhai Lake and Dali

### Xizhou (喜洲)
**What to do:** walk the historic lanes and courtyard-house area, visit the market if open, and look for Bai architectural features in context. Ask the guide which courtyard or heritage site is open rather than assuming all historic-looking houses are public attractions.

**Food:** try **Xizhou baba**, a local baked flatbread with savoury or sweet fillings. A market stop is also a practical way to sample snacks and see local produce. Ask about ingredients if you have dietary restrictions.

**Context:** Xizhou is a Bai settlement in the Erhai basin. Its houses, market and food traditions are better understood as parts of village life than as a staged cultural display.

### Zhoucheng (周城)
**What to do:** if a tie-dye workshop is operating, ask for a demonstration of the resist-dye process and whether visitors can try it. Ask how the pattern is made and whether the cloth is naturally or synthetically dyed; don't infer the method just from the finished colour.

**Buy thoughtfully:** buying directly from a maker can support local craft, but clarify whether a product is handmade locally and what the price includes. Ask before photographing workers.

### Erhai Lake (洱海)
**What to do:** cycle only the confirmed route, stopping at designated pull-offs and lakeside villages. Look for the balance between lake ecology, agriculture, village life and tourism infrastructure—not only the mountain reflection.

**Risks:** don't take photos while cycling; pull fully off the route. Confirm helmets, traffic exposure, route distance, bike condition and the return time before setting out. Don't assume every lakeside path is continuous or separated from road traffic.

### Dali Old Town (大理古城)
**What to do:** walk the old-town street grid, explore the market and lanes, and consider the Three Pagodas if the operator's schedule permits and tickets/access are confirmed. The 15-day PDF does not explicitly include the Three Pagodas, so this is an optional addition, not a guaranteed stop.

**History:** the Erhai basin was the political heartland of the Nanzhao kingdom and later the Dali Kingdom, founded in 937. The region connected southwest China with wider trade and cultural routes.

**Food:** try **rushan** (a Bai goat-milk cheese often grilled or served in other preparations), **Xizhou baba**, **three-course tea** if offered with a clear explanation of the ritual, and Yunnan rice noodles. Ask about allergens and preparation methods.

## 20.10 Kunming: Stone Forest and Dounan Flower Market

### Stone Forest / Shilin (石林)
**What to do:** prioritize the main rock formations and the route the operator confirms; the park is large enough that the exact zone matters. The official tourism site identifies several sub-areas, so ask whether the visit includes Big Stone Forest, Small Stone Forest or another section.

**History and geology:** the formations are karst limestone shaped over geological time by dissolution and erosion. The Stone Forest is also associated with Yi/Sani cultural interpretation, including the Ashima story; distinguish local cultural narrative from geological explanation.

**Food:** if the schedule allows, look for Yunnan rice noodles, grilled local snacks and simple restaurant food near the scenic area. Because this is a transfer-heavy excursion, ask the operator whether lunch is planned and where. Don't rely on an assumed restaurant stop.

**Risks:** paths and stone steps can be slippery; wear shoes with grip and avoid climbing on rock formations. Keep the group meeting point and return time clear. The drive from Kunming is substantial, so confirm the plan for returning in time for Dounan.

### Dounan Flower Market (斗南花卉市场)
**What to do:** observe the difference between retail flower displays and wholesale trade—sorting, packing, trolleys, auctions or buying activity depending on the time. Ask whether your visit is intended for the retail hall or the busier wholesale period.

**Timing:** a current travel listing says retail hours are generally 09:00–18:00 and wholesale activity is liveliest around 20:00–02:00. That timing may not fit the 15-day PDF's planned afternoon visit; confirm what is open at the actual arrival time.

**Photography and safety:** don't block aisles or loading routes, touch stock without permission, or photograph workers at close range without consent. Watch for trolleys and vehicles in busy market areas.

**Food:** the market is not primarily a destination for a long sit-down meal. Plan food around the Stone Forest excursion or the hotel/transport schedule; use the market for the visual and commercial experience.
